1:40:37: Penrose gives what for me sounds like a convincing resolution of the EPR situation. The only quibble I have, is that there is no time at all in what he calls "quantum reality" (describable only with timeless mathematics). That's not the same as "going backwards in the light cone". I think his description of Bob's position is correct anyway! Note that the light cone is a construction that involves both space and time. Its not in the Platonic World. :-)

I slightly disagree with Penrose about how to think about the eternity of mathematics. He thinks its simply all out there waiting to be discovered, I think mathematics evolves  (relative to our perceptions) but once discovered becomes part of eternal truth, beyond time.
This doesn't affect anything significantly, its just a quibble about the best way to think about things. (I'm assuming that temporality -- what we experience as The Arrow of Time -- is intrinsic to the Universe.)

My answer to Levin would be that "Space and Time are a brain strategy for reducing complexity." Platonic objects are therefore inaccessible to brains that think in terms of space and time. Parts of  the (real) Platonic World can, however be modelled using mathematics that describes/suggests real experiments (That's what physics does). Other parts can be described (modelled) using ordinary language ("I like coffee."). Yet more parts can be modelled using the Arts: music, poetry etc...
My old definition of Art (1970s): "Art is the expression of insight." Apropos Einstein's criterium for reality: If I can connect a poetically expressed ideas with something in my experience, than that poetry has communicated something real.

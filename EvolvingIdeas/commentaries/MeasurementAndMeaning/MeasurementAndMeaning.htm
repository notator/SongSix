Measurement

A note on mathematics:
As described in the section about mathematics in The Arrow of Time, I think of mathematics as an evolving tool that we can use to model the physical Universe. The models describe what we perceive, either directly with our senses, or indirectly via additional physical apparatus.
Note carefully that:
  1. The models are not the physical reality they are modelling.
  2. Pure mathematics is not restricted by having to describe physical reality.
  3. The science of physics is concerned with those aspects of physical reality that are amenable to mathematical modelling.
  4. Mathematical models suggest physical experiments that test their veracity (Popper: Conjectures and Refutations). The models continue to evolve because successful experiments change the context in which new ideas emerge.
There is, of course, more to reality than the physical reality we perceive with our physical senses (Kastrup: Dashboard hypothesis), but measurement is always done on this side of the dashboard, using our senses.
We are talking here about everyday experience. There is nothing mysterious going on.

Physical Apparatus

Telescopes, sextants, vernier scales, micrometers etc. have been used to improve the accuracy our unaided senses, since long before the 20th century. These devices can be read to a maximum accuracy of about 5 significant figures. Integers and multiples or ratios of 6-figure integers are therefore sufficient for this purpose. Infinite precision (so-called "real") numbers are never required.  For example: pi ( π ) is never the result of a physical measurement. Irrational numbers ( x + iy ) constructed from two real numbers and an operator ( i ), are of course also never observed.

Physical reality:
Definition: Something is physically real if it can be associated with a physical quantity (a number) that can be predicted in advance.
This is a paraphrase of the Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen "criterion of reality" quoted in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy as follows:
“If, without in any way disturbing a system, we can predict with certainty (i.e., with probability equal to unity) the value of a physical quantity, then there exists an element of reality corresponding to that quantity.”
Notice that the prediction is always related to measurements, and that measurements are always made in the measurer's space and time.
The paradoxes associated with the EPR criterion can therefore be resolved as follows: Experiments have shown that while locality is physically real on the side of the dashboard of perception in which we measure physical quantities, it is non-locality that is physically real on the other side of the dashboard. This does not contradict the experiments. Its just an artefact of the dashboard.
Physicists' spacetime is a mathematical model containing spatial and temporal components. These models are not the space and time themselves in which we perceive ourselves to exist. The space and time in which we perceive ourselves to exist are the result of a brain strategy for reducing complexity.

Existence
Definition: There are three types of existence:
  • Physical: Physically instantiated sense organs provide information about physically existing objects and events beyond the body of which they are part. Such information allows actions to be initiated (food gathering, avoiding predators etc.) that are important for the body's survival.
  • Conceptual: The brain processes raw physical experiences (sense data) into higher level concepts whose existence promotes efficiency. The ability to form such concepts also leads to speculative fantasies (such as atoms or unicorns) that are not immediately necessary for survival.
  • Transcendent: The parts of reality that exist (beyond the dashboard), but that are beyond our capacity to conceive.

Context The information that is required for understanding the meaning of a symbol or the evolution of a situation.
Existence There are three distinct types of existence:
  • Physical: Physically instantiated sense organs provide information about physically existing objects and events beyond the body of which they are part. Such information allows actions to be initiated (food gathering, avoiding predators etc.) that are important for the body's survival.
  • Conceptual: The brain processes raw physical experiences (sense data) into higher level concepts whose existence promotes efficiency. Such concepts also lead to speculative fantasies (such as atoms or unicorns) that are not immediately necessary for survival.
  • Transcendent: There are parts of reality that exist (beyond the dashboard), that are beyond our capacity to conceive.
Physically Real Something is physically real if it can be associated with a physical quantity (a number) that can be predicted in advance.
(This definition is closely related to the EPR "criterion of reality".)
